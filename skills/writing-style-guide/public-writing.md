# Public and personal writing

Use this for social posts, bios, cover letters, personal statements, and anything published in the user's own voice.

The writing should sound like the user, not like a template. Specific details make it theirs: a moment, a number, a name, a reason. Generic sentiment ("I've learned so much", "excited for what's next") could appear in anyone's post, so cut it or replace it with something only this person could say.

When you don't know the specifics, put the `[placeholders]` where the specifics go and keep the surrounding text plain. A draft that writes out the generic parts and leaves the personal parts blank has it backwards.

Avoid the genre's clichés: "humbled", "journey", "after much reflection", "thrilled to announce", a stack of single-line paragraphs building to a reveal, a wall of hashtags, and emojis unless the user writes that way. Thank specific people for specific things, or leave thanks out.

For cover letters and personal statements, show fit with evidence: what the user did and what came of it, matched to what the role needs. Adjectives about themselves ("passionate", "detail-oriented") prove nothing.

Write 1 draft. Offer alternatives only if the user asks for them.

## Example

The user asked for a short LinkedIn post announcing they're leaving their job to start a company.

Flawed:

> It's a hard decision. I've learned a lot here, worked with people I admire, and I'm proud of what we built together. Thank you to my team, my mentors, and everyone who took a chance on me along the way.
>
> For a while now I've been thinking about [the problem you want to solve], and I couldn't shake the idea. So I'm going all in.
>
> My last day at [Company] is [date]. Excited for what's next.

Every sentence outside the brackets could be in anyone's post, and the dramatic "So I'm going all in" turn is a genre habit.

Preferred:

> After [X years] at [Company], my last day is [date]. I'm leaving to start [Company name], which [one-line description of what you're building].
>
> I've wanted to fix [the problem] since [the moment you ran into it]. [One sentence on why now.]
>
> Thank you to [specific people or team], especially for [something specific they did].
>
> If you've dealt with [the problem], I'd like to talk to you.
